# Preliminary F1/F2 vowel-coarticulation reanalysis — 2026-09-05

This supersedes the earlier duration-focused exploratory analysis. The target outcome is **context-induced displacement of vowel F1/F2**, with three derived dimensions: **magnitude**, **temporal extent**, and **flexibility**.

## 1. Mitra & Dutta Bengali–English: C→V place effect

Clean minimal onset contrasts were constructed from `bad~dad`, `buck~duck`, and `bug~dug`, holding vowel and coda constant. Repetitions were averaged within speaker × task × language-context × pair before computing `/d/ onset − /b/ onset`. N=10 speakers.

### F2

| Vowel time | /d/ − /b/ F2 |
|---:|---:|
| 5% | +382.93 Hz |
| 15% | +268.56 Hz |
| 25% | +220.38 Hz |
| 35% | +166.81 Hz |
| 45% | +123.13 Hz |

Speaker-specific temporal slope: **−6.21 Hz per percentage point**, t=−17.33, p=3.20e−8. This is a very strong onset-place coarticulatory effect: alveolar /d/ raises early-vowel F2 relative to bilabial /b/, and the effect decays but remains present through 45% of the vowel.

### F1

/d/ − /b/ F1 was −29.27 Hz at 5%, −34.75 at 15%, −26.56 at 25%, −20.34 at 35%, and −12.93 at 45%. Pointwise speaker tests are significant at each sampled time point, but the speaker-specific linear decay slope is not robust at N=10 (p≈.095).

### Flexibility

The F2 onset-place magnitude at the earliest measurement is about **46.6 Hz larger in the sentence/code-switching task than picture naming** (speaker-level p≈.040). There is no comparable evidence that mixed Bengali vs nonswitch English context changes the onset-place effect. Treat this task interaction as preliminary because N=10.

## 2. Cohn & Zellou California English: following-consonant effect

The clean comparison `CVN − CVC` holds the /b/ onset and vowel constant while comparing nasal /n/ vs oral /d/ codas. N=27 speakers, five within-vowel time points.

### F1

/n/ − /d/ coda effect:

- 21.45%: −7.07 Hz (p=.20)
- 35.75%: −1.00 Hz (p=.87)
- 50.05%: +9.41 Hz (p=.15)
- 64.35%: **+25.03 Hz** (p=.00055)
- 78.65%: **+36.63 Hz** (p=.000016)

Speaker-specific slope: **+0.793 Hz per percentage point**, p=2.16e−8. The F1 effect therefore emerges strongly in the latter half of the vowel and grows toward the coda: clear anticipatory contextual structure in vowel formants.

### F2

/n/ − /d/ coda effect is already positive early and peaks near the midpoint: +47.60, +57.32, +62.64, +52.48, +23.39 Hz across the five time points. The temporal slope is modestly negative (−0.373 Hz per percentage point, p≈.040), so this is not a simple late-growing anticipatory F2 pattern.

### Carryover onset contrast

`NVN − CVN` compares /m/ vs /b/ onsets while holding the /n/ coda fixed. F1 changes from about +17.7 Hz early to −15.1 Hz late; speaker-specific slope **−0.601 Hz per percentage point**, p=4.91e−5. This is a robust time-varying carryover effect of onset manner/nasality on vowel F1. F2 is roughly +29–39 Hz across much of the vowel but its temporal slope is not significant.

### Flexibility

For the coda comparison, the F2 trajectory differs between fast-clear and casual speech (difference in temporal slope ≈ +0.542 Hz per percentage point, p≈.027); slow-clear vs casual is not robust in this F1/F2 contrast. Thus style flexibility is detectable in formant trajectory, even apart from the study's nasalization measure.

## 3. Chaturvedi & Shaw American English: V→V effect in joint F1/F2 space

Four vowel-pair datasets with 11 trajectory samples per token were pooled. Within speaker and time point, log F1/F2 were standardized using correctly produced control tokens. For every critical non-error token, an assimilation index was computed as projection of the observed F1/F2 point from the target-vowel control centroid toward the competing-vowel centroid. Index 0 means no shift; positive values mean movement toward the contextual competitor. N=12 speakers.

### Anticipatory V→V

The anticipatory assimilation trajectory has a significant positive speaker-specific slope (**+0.118 over the normalized vowel**, p=.00036). The mean index is near or below zero through the middle but becomes positive at the vowel edge:

- 80%: **+0.0428**, p=.0044
- 90%: **+0.0508**, p=.036
- 100%: **+0.0894**, p=.042

This is important because a midpoint-only analysis would largely miss the effect. It provides a direct example of **temporal extent**: anticipatory V→V coarticulation is concentrated late in the target vowel.

### Carryover V→V

The corresponding perseveratory/carryover trajectory is weak and not robust (speaker-specific slope p≈.455), so the present data support anticipatory but not clear carryover assimilation in joint F1/F2 space.

## Interpretation

The F1/F2 data support the three-part decomposition:

1. **Magnitude** — e.g. the /d/ vs /b/ onset contrast produces ~383 Hz F2 displacement at 5% into the vowel.
2. **Temporal extent** — the C→V place effect decays across the vowel; the Cohn coda F1 effect grows toward the coda; Chaturvedi anticipatory V→V assimilation emerges only near the vowel end.
3. **Flexibility** — formant coarticulation can vary with speaking/task context; current evidence is strongest for Mitra–Dutta task effects and Cohn–Zellou fast-clear F2 trajectory effects.

These analyses are preliminary source-level reanalyses. A pooled language-level model should wait until more studies are harmonized with speaker, vowel, adjacent segment, F1/F2, and normalized time retained separately from study/source.

## Discovery: IFCASL

Added `ifcasl_2016` to HIGH-priority review. Trouvain et al. (2016) describe a primary French-German bilingual learner/native production corpus with about 100 speakers, word- and phone-level segmentation, and more than 50% manually corrected data. The corpus has been used for segmental realization, final devoicing, vowel quantity/quality, pitch range, tempo, pauses, and other phonetic-production analyses.

Reason for review rather than acceptance: current discovery verified the paper and project pages plus corpus samples, but did not locate a stable public token-level annotation/measurement deposit with complete reusable files and clear file-level redistribution terms. Manuality is sufficiently promising to preserve the candidate, but public-data ingestability remains unresolved.

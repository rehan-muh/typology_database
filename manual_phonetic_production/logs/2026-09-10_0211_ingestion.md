### Puggaard-Rode 2023 — Jutland Danish /t/ release

High-value candidate located at the author's public reproducibility site and GitHub repository. The study explicitly reports 5,169 /t/ releases manually segmented from waveform landmarks, with burst and onset of periodicity delimiting the release. Public `data/affric.csv` has 10,447 stop-token rows and variables `parish`, `token`, `cog`, `vot`, `pal`, `height`, `backness`, `round`, `stop`, `stress`, `.obs`; public participant/geographic files are also exposed. The recordings themselves are a legacy 1971–1976 Danish dialect corpus, so this source must be handled under the secondary-recording exception: only the newly produced, isolatable manually segmented /t/ layer qualifies. Next pass should inventory repository license/commit, filter and count `stop=t`, preserve the complete source table by immutable reference/checksum, build the variable dictionary, and ingest the qualifying /t/ rows plus participant linkage.

### Culhane et al. 2026 — te reo Māori opening vowel sequences

New Laboratory Phonology paper screened. Public supplement exposes large token/trajectory files including raw formant and pitch/intensity trajectories. The study reports manual transcription and new manual morpheme-boundary coding, but MAONZE phonetic data are described as force aligned; no evidence of exhaustive manual correction of the relevant vowel boundaries was established in this pass. Therefore no phonetic observations were promoted to core. Retain as a review lead only if later documentation establishes human verification of the intervals used for acoustic extraction.

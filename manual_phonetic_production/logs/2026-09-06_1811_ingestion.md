## Accepted

### `indian_stroke_speech_2025_2026`

- **Corpus/publication:** Indian stroke speech corpus; Jyothi et al. (2026), *Characterizing stroke-affected speech using F0 and duration-based features*.
- **Repository:** Zenodo record 15675907; dataset DOI 10.5281/zenodo.15675906.
- **Primary collection:** yes. Speech was recorded in the AIIMS Mangalagiri stroke ward from 50 stroke patients and 50 healthy control speakers; all participants are Telugu L1 speakers.
- **Tasks:** five sustained vowels /A E I O U/, each repeated twice; three-word phonetically rich read sentences.
- **Manual provenance:** the publication explicitly states that raw recordings were manually segmented to produce independent vowel-category utterances and that sentence recordings were segmented similarly.
- **Measures documented:** instantaneous F0 contours and F0 mean/median/SD; z-normalized F0; transition/non-transition timing measures including RTD, RTA, TR and analogous steady-state quantities.
- **Important provenance split:** transition regions themselves are algorithmically detected from cepstral-difference trajectories and were not manually validated in the reported study. They may be retained as automatic derived measurements attached to manually segmented utterances, but must not be represented as manual segment boundaries.
- **Status:** accepted reference layer; Zenodo file-level inventory/checksums/license still pending.

## Discovery decisions

- Large corpora whose human annotation is only orthographic/transcript-level continue to be rejected for the manually grounded phonetic core unless a separable manually corrected phonetic tier exists.
- Automatic transition/feature extraction is permitted as a derived measurement **only when attached to a qualifying manually grounded production unit**; its provenance must remain explicitly automatic.
- No singing or synthetic-speech sources were admitted.

# Ingestion log — 2026-09-17 14:12 PT

## Backlog ingestion

Accepted `ekskfk_2023_v1_3` (Phonetic Corpus of Estonian Spontaneous Speech v1.3; DOI 10.23673/re-438) as a primary non-singing manually annotated production corpus. Official v1.3 documentation reports 135 hours, 207 speakers (ages 20–85), and manual word/phoneme annotation for 106 hours / approximately one million word intervals. Official historical META-SHARE documentation states that segmentation was manually performed in Praat and documents word, phoneme (Estonian-adjusted SAMPA), syllable, prosodic-foot, intonation-phrase/IPU, and voice-quality tiers. These are admissible manual production annotations.

Added a source-level manifest for publicly listed v1.3 objects, including `EKSKFK_v1-3_words-by-IPU.txt` (12.48 MB), `EKSKFK_doc.zip` (22.21 KB), respiratory TextGrid/WAV archives, and large OpenPose JSON archives. Automatic OpenPose products are explicitly excluded from the manual core. Large waveform archives remain authoritative-reference-only rather than being committed to GitHub.

Added a six-row source-faithful variable dictionary for the documented manual annotation tiers. Original Estonian-adjusted SAMPA is to be retained; IPA mapping must be separate and only when explicitly defensible.

Added lineage rules preventing double counting across EKSKFK v1.0.0–v1.3. v1.3 is the preferred current release; older versions are provenance/history rather than independent corpora unless genuinely non-overlapping observations are demonstrated.

## Discovery / audit

Fresh searches across manual TextGrid, manual segmentation, manual/corrected formant, production, Zenodo/OSF/repository terminology identified EKSKFK as the strongest new qualifying source in this pass. It is unusually broad because it combines spontaneous primary speech with manual segmental, prosodic, and voice-quality annotation. Existing L2-ARCTIC and Bortlik targets resurfaced and were not duplicated. Singing collections were excluded by scope. GRAIN was not admitted because it is based on radio interviews/third-party broadcast material and therefore violates the primary-recording criterion despite a manual gold subset.
